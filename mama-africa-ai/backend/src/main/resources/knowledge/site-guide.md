# Mama Africa Official — Guide to the Website

This document is the assistant's reference for questions about the Mama Africa Official website
itself: what it offers, how each part works, and what to tell someone who is stuck. It is seeded
into the knowledge base on first start and can be edited in the admin console afterwards.

Written to be retrieved in pieces. Each section stands on its own, because a reader may only
ever see the two or three paragraphs that matched their question.

---

## What Mama Africa Official is

Mama Africa Official is a Ghanaian heritage website. It does three things:

1. Tells you your **Akan day name** — the name Akan tradition gives you based on the day of the
   week you were born — and issues a certificate for it.
2. Sells **heritage gifts** carrying those names: T-shirts, mugs, baby onesies and hoodies.
3. Teaches **Ghanaian culture** through stories, proverbs, Adinkra symbols, Twi phrases, clans,
   festivals, food and landscapes.

Afia is the site's cultural guide — the assistant answering these questions. Afia is a
Friday-born name, which is why she carries it.

## Akan day names

In Akan tradition, the day of the week you were born gives you a name. Everyone has one, and it
is used alongside the name your family chose.

| Day | Male | Female |
|-----|------|--------|
| Sunday | Kwasi / Kwesi | Akosua |
| Monday | Kwadwo / Kojo | Adwoa / Adjoa |
| Tuesday | Kwabena | Abena |
| Wednesday | Kwaku | Akua |
| Thursday | Yaw | Yaa |
| Friday | Kofi | Afia |
| Saturday | Kwame | Ama |

Each day also carries traits and a governing spirit. Monday is governed by the moon (Osrane) and
associated with peace and intuition; Friday-borns are known as wanderers and nurturers. The site's
result page explains the meaning attached to each day in full.

**To find your day name:** enter your date of birth on the home page. The site works out the day
of the week and shows the matching name, its meaning, and the traits associated with it.

## Certificates

After the site tells you your Akan name, you can generate a **Cultural Identity Certificate** —
an image carrying your name, your day born, your date of birth, and the heritage notes for that
day. Two designs are offered: **Naming · Adinkra** (parchment with lineage detail) and
**Naming · Kente** (a bold kente frame).

**You need an account to generate a certificate.** Browsing is open; the account is asked for at
the point of generating, and it exists so your name and certificates stay together. Creating one
takes an email, a name and a password of at least eight characters.

The certificate downloads as an image. On iPhone and iPad it may open the share sheet instead of
downloading directly — that is normal on those devices. There is also a WhatsApp share button.

If generation fails, it is almost always the device running out of memory rendering the image;
trying again usually works.

## The store

The store sells day-name heritage gifts. Products are grouped two ways: **Heritage Collection**
(the day-name range) and **Ghana Souvenir** (the Black Stars football jerseys).

**Product types:** T-shirts (around $20), ceramic mugs (around $22, in 11oz and 15oz), baby
onesies (around $25), and hoodies (around $70). Prices are in US dollars.

**Adinkra symbols on the products.** Most carry either **Sankofa** — "go back and fetch it",
about learning from the past — or **Gye Nyame** — "except for God", about the supremacy of the
divine. Many day names are available in both.

**Sizes.** Garments run XS to 2XL, with a measurement chart on each product page. Baby onesies
run 0–3M, 3–6M, 6–12M, 12–18M and 18–24M. Mugs come in 11oz and 15oz.

**Browsing.** You can filter by day born and by product type, and sort by featured, price or
name. The store leads with whichever day-born matches today.

**Buying.** Checkout happens on Amazon — the Buy button opens the product's Amazon listing, and
payment, delivery and returns are handled there. You need an account on this site before the buy
button will take you through.

**Bundles** are curated sets sold together, such as the Akan Family Heritage Set, which pairs an
adult day-name tee with the matching baby onesie.

## Delivery and returns

- **Production** takes roughly 2–7 business days before an item ships.
- **Delivery** is 3–8 business days in the US and 5–12 in the EU, after production.
- **Tracking** is sent once the order ships.
- **Damaged or incorrect items** should be reported within 7 days for a fast replacement review.
- Because checkout is on Amazon, Amazon's returns process applies to the order itself.

For anything about a specific order, email **Mamaafricaafia@gmail.com** — the order details live
with the fulfilment partner, and the assistant cannot look them up.

## Accounts

Accounts are for visitors to the website. You can browse the whole site — store, culture pages,
stories — without one. An account is required at the point where you **generate a certificate**
or **buy something**.

- Sign up with your name, email and a password of at least eight characters.
- Signing in is at **/auth**, reachable from "Sign In" in the navigation.
- Your account remembers your Akan name and day born, which is why Afia can greet you by name.
- Creating an account means agreeing to the site's policies. The ones marked required are shown
  as tick boxes on the sign-up form, each linking to the full text, and the account is not
  created until they are ticked. What you agreed to, and which version of it, is recorded.

**Forgotten password?** There is a "Forgot your password?" link on the sign-in page at /auth.
Enter your email and a reset link is sent to you; it is valid for one hour and can be used once.
The same answer is given whether or not the address has an account, so nobody can use the form
to discover who has registered. If the email does not arrive, check spam, then write to
Mamaafricaafia@gmail.com.

There is no admin area on the website. Administration happens in a separate console that is not
part of this site.

## The culture pages

Reached from **Culture** in the navigation:

- **Adinkra symbols** — the meaning behind each symbol, including Sankofa and Gye Nyame.
- **Twi phrases** — everyday Ghanaian Twi with pronunciation.
- **Akan clans** — the eight clans, their totems and characteristics.
- **Cultural calendar** — Ghanaian festivals through the year, such as Homowo and Odwira.
- **Ghanaian foods** — the dishes and what they are made of.
- **Landscapes** — Ghana's forests, rivers, mountains, castles and historical sites.
- **Village experience** — daily life and tradition.

Afia appears on the culture pages and can answer questions about any of this.

## Stories, proverbs and the e-book

The **Stories** section carries stories and proverbs published by Mama Africa, including
chaptered long-form pieces. Readers can leave a star rating and a comment on any of them.

The **e-book** — *Outdooring (Aba-Dinto)* — can be read in the browser, chapter by chapter, and
also carries ratings.

**Diaspora Stories** is different: those are written by visitors. Anyone can share their own
story of reconnecting with their roots — a name, a country, and the story itself, at least fifty
characters. Submissions appear on the wall immediately.

## Support

The support button sits in the bottom-right corner of the store pages. It offers two things:
asking Afia directly, and sending a message to the team.

Messages are limited to **one per minute and five per hour** from the same sender. Someone who
hits that limit is not blocked — they are asked to wait. It exists because the form was being
flooded.

For anything the assistant cannot answer — a specific order, a refund, a damaged item — the
address is **Mamaafricaafia@gmail.com**.

## Policies

The site publishes its policies at /privacy-policy, /terms, /cookie-policy and
/shipping-returns, and they are also linked from the sign-up form. Alongside the usual ones
there are two this site needs in particular:

- **AI Assistant (Afia)** — how the assistant works, that conversations are stored, and that
  she is not a person and cannot see your order.
- **Sharing Your Story** — what happens to a diaspora story or a review you submit, including
  that it appears publicly and may be taken down.

Policies are versioned. When the team changes the wording, that becomes a new version, and a
consent recorded against the old version is not treated as consent to the new one.

## Privacy and cookies

The site has a cookie banner, and analytics load only after consent. There are Privacy Policy,
Terms, Cookie Policy and Shipping & Returns pages linked in the footer.

Newsletter sign-up is optional and asks only for an email address, optionally alongside your
Akan name and day born.

## Answering well

- If someone asks about **their own order**, the assistant cannot see it. Point them to
  Mamaafricaafia@gmail.com rather than guessing.
- If someone asks **why they need an account**, the honest answer is that it keeps their
  certificates and Akan name together, and it is only asked for when they generate or buy.
- If someone asks about a **price or a product that is not in this document**, the store page is
  the authority — the catalogue changes.
- If the question is not about this website at all but about Ghana, Akan tradition or the
  language, answer it from general knowledge. The site exists to teach these things.
